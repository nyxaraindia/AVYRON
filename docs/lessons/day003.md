## 2. Beginner Explanation (ELI5)

Imagine a robot moving inside a room.

It encounters a box blocking its path.

What should it do?

The robot needs to:

1. Detect the box.
2. Understand that the box is blocking its path.
3. Decide what to do.
4. Move around the box.
5. Check whether it successfully avoided the box.

This process is called the Robot Intelligence Loop.

A robot should not simply execute commands without considering what is happening around it.

It should continuously observe its environment, make decisions and examine the results of its actions.

## 3. The Five Stages of the Intelligence Loop

### 3.1 Perception

Perception means collecting information from the environment or the robot itself.

Examples:

- Camera detects an object.
- LiDAR measures distance.
- Microphone receives sound.
- IMU measures orientation and acceleration.
- Force sensor measures applied force.

Main question:

"What information am I receiving?"

Example:

A camera detects a red object.

The camera has collected information, but the robot may not yet know what the object means.

### 3.2 Understanding

Understanding means interpreting the information collected through perception.

Examples:

- Recognising an object as a box.
- Identifying that a box blocks a path.
- Estimating whether a person is approaching.
- Understanding that the robot is running low on battery.

Main question:

"What is happening?"

Example:

The robot identifies the detected box as an obstacle.

Important:

Perception and understanding are different.

Detecting an object does not automatically mean understanding its meaning correctly.

Sensor information can be noisy, incomplete or incorrect.

### 3.3 Decision

Decision-making means selecting an appropriate action based on:

- Current understanding.
- Desired goal.
- Available capabilities.
- Physical limitations.
- Environmental conditions.
- Safety constraints.
- Available resources.

Main question:

"What should I do?"

Example:

The robot decides to stop and calculate another route.

A decision should not be considered safe merely because the intelligence system selected it. It must also satisfy the applicable safety requirements.

### 3.4 Action

Action means physically or computationally executing the selected decision.

Examples:

- Moving a motor.
- Rotating a robotic joint.
- Opening a gripper.
- Turning a mobile robot.
- Changing a drone's flight direction.

Main question:

"How should I execute the decision?"

Example:

The robot sends suitable commands to its motors to turn left.

### 3.5 Feedback

Feedback means observing what happened after executing an action.

Examples:

- Checking whether the robot moved.
- Measuring its new position.
- Checking whether the obstacle was avoided.
- Detecting unexpected movement.
- Checking whether the task was completed.

Main question:

"Did my action produce the expected result?"

Feedback helps the robot update its understanding and make better subsequent decisions.

## 4. The Intelligence Loop

The five stages form a continuous process:

Perception
    |
    v
Understanding
    |
    v
Decision
    |
    v
Action
    |
    v
Feedback
    |
    v
Updated Understanding
    |
    v
Repeat

The loop does not necessarily operate as five strictly separated sequential steps in every robotics architecture. Some systems process information and control actions continuously or in parallel.

However, this model provides a useful foundation for understanding robot intelligence.

## 5. Mathematical Foundations

Let:

S_t = Sensor information at time t

W_t = Robot's estimated understanding of the world

G = Robot's goal

A_t = Selected action at time t

F_t = Feedback received after the action

### 5.1 Understanding

W_t = Understand(S_t)

The robot uses sensor information to estimate what is happening.

### 5.2 Decision

A_t = Decide(W_t, G)

The robot selects an action using its current understanding and goal.

### 5.3 Feedback

F_t = ObserveResult(A_t)

The robot observes the result of its action.

### 5.4 Updating Understanding

W_(t+1) = Update(W_t, F_t)

The robot updates its understanding using new feedback.

These equations are conceptual representations, not implemented AVYRON algorithms.

## 6. Computational Logic

A simplified algorithm:

START

1. Receive sensor information.
2. Interpret the information.
3. Check the current goal.
4. Generate possible actions.
5. Evaluate the available actions.
6. Select an appropriate action.
7. Check applicable safety constraints.
8. Execute an authorized action.
9. Observe the result.
10. Update the understanding.
11. Repeat.

END

Important:

If an action violates an enforced safety constraint, it must not be executed merely because the planner selected it.

## 7. Worked Example: Avoiding an Obstacle

Situation:

A mobile robot wants to reach a destination.

It detects a box in front of it.

### Stage 1 — Perception

The camera detects the box.

### Stage 2 — Understanding

The robot estimates that the box is blocking its current path.

### Stage 3 — Decision

The robot considers stopping, turning left or turning right.

### Stage 4 — Action

The robot executes an authorized movement command.

### Stage 5 — Feedback

The robot checks its new position and whether the box has been avoided.

If the robot has not avoided the box, it updates its understanding and replans.

## 8. Changing Conditions Example

Situation:

A robot is travelling towards a charging station.

Initial conditions:

- Battery: 8%
- Distance to charging station: 5 metres
- Obstacle: Detected
- Goal: Reach the charging station safely

The robot detects the obstacle, estimates its position, selects an appropriate route and begins moving.

But suppose the battery suddenly drops to 2%.

The robot may need to reconsider its plan.

This demonstrates why intelligence must consider changing conditions rather than relying on a single decision made earlier.

The intelligence loop connects several major robotics disciplines.

|---|---|

| Understanding | State estimation, object recognition, world modelling |
| Decision | AI planning, task planning, decision theory |
| Action | Motion planning, control systems, motor control |
| Feedback | Localization, state estimation, control feedback |

These are broad technical areas, not complete implementations.

This architecture is a research proposal. It has not yet been implemented or experimentally validated.

### Important Research Principle

The intelligence system must not be allowed to bypass independent safety enforcement.

The planner can propose actions, but an independently enforced safety mechanism must determine whether the relevant actions are authorized.
