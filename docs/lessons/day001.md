# AVYRON — Day 1
## Foundations of Robotics Intelligence

### Learning Objective

Understand what a robot is, what robotic intelligence means,
why different robots need different capabilities, and what AVYRON
aims to investigate.

## 1. What is a robot?

A robot is a physical or virtual machine that can sense information,
process it and perform actions.

- Sensors: observe the environment.
- Computing system: processes information.
- Decision system: chooses actions.
- Actuators: perform physical actions.

## 2. What is robotic intelligence?

Imagine asking a robot to bring you a glass of water.

A fixed program may follow a predetermined sequence.
An adaptive system should observe the situation, choose a suitable
strategy and respond to changes.

A simplified intelligence cycle:

1. Perceive
2. Understand
3. Decide
4. Act
5. Observe the result
6. Adapt

## 3. The universal robotics problem

A humanoid, robotic arm and drone have different bodies,
sensors, actuators and physical limits.

The instruction "pick up the box" cannot be executed identically
by all three.

A drone without a gripper cannot pick up a box merely because
the intelligence system understands the instruction.

The architecture must understand both the goal and the robot's
actual capabilities.

## 4. Five learning categories

These are teaching categories, not a universal intelligence ranking.

1. Reactive: responds to something.
2. Planned: follows a sequence.
3. Adaptive: changes strategy.
4. Learning: improves from experience.
5. Generalizable: transfers knowledge to new situations.

## 5. AVYRON research ambition

Investigate a shared intelligence architecture that can understand
tasks, model robot capabilities, select feasible strategies,
respect safety constraints, execute and monitor actions,
adapt to changing conditions and learn through controlled validation.

This is a research ambition, not a proven result.

## 6. Day 1 Research Question

what information must it know about each robot before assigning a task?

Write your own answer.
