## 1. What Is a Robot?

A robot is a physical machine capable of performing actions in the physical world.

Five basic functional components:

1. Sensors — collect information.
2. Computing hardware — processes information.
3. Intelligence system — interprets information and selects actions.
4. Actuators — produce physical movement.
5. Power system — supplies energy.

## 2. How Robotic Intelligence Works

The basic cycle:

1. Perceive — collect information.
2. Understand — interpret information.
3. Decide — select an action.
4. Act — execute the action.
5. Observe again — verify the result.

This is a feedback loop.

Example: A robotic arm identifies a red box, plans its movement,
grasps the box and checks whether the grasp succeeded.

If the box slips, the system must detect the failure and respond.

## 3. Different Robot Embodiments

### Humanoid
- Can potentially walk and manipulate objects.
- May have arms, hands or grippers.
- Must maintain balance.

### Robotic Arm
- Can reach and manipulate objects.
- Has a limited workspace.
- Has payload, force and torque limits.

### Drone
- Can fly in three-dimensional space.
- Has limited payload and flight time.
- May not have a gripper.

### Quadruped
- Moves using four legs.
- Must maintain balance.

### Wheeled Robot
- Moves using wheels.
- Can be limited by stairs and rough terrain.

The same task cannot necessarily be performed by every robot.

## 4. Categories of Robotic Behaviour

1. Reactive — responds to events.
2. Planned — creates action sequences.
3. Adaptive — changes strategies.
4. Learning — improves through experience.
5. Generalizable — transfers knowledge to new situations.

These are conceptual categories, not a universal ranking.

Learning must never bypass independently enforced safety constraints.

## 6. First Research Principle

### Capability-Aware Task Assignment

Before assigning a task, AVYRON must compare the task's requirements
against the robot's verified capabilities and current operating conditions.

A robot's appearance does not prove that it can perform a task.

## 7. Six Capability Checks

The learner identified six important areas:

1. Hands or gripper capability.
2. Vision and object recognition.
3. Movement and reachability.
4. Strength and lifting capacity.
5. Environmental conditions.
6. Safety constraints.

Other factors also matter, including energy, payload, force, torque,
workspace, calibration and robot condition.

## 8. Payload Reasoning Exercise

Task: Pick up an 8 kg box and place it on a table.

Given:

| Robot | Lifting capacity |
|---|---:|
| Humanoid | 5 kg |
| Robotic arm | 15 kg |
| Drone | 2 kg |

### Humanoid

8 - 5 = 3 kg above its stated lifting capacity.

Decision: Reject the lifting action under the given limit.

### Drone

8 - 2 = 6 kg above its stated lifting capacity.

Decision: Reject the lifting action under the given limit.

### Robotic Arm

15 - 8 = 7 kg difference.

Decision: Suitable candidate for further verification.

The robotic arm must also pass checks for:
- Gripper suitability.
- Reachability.
- Workspace.
- Force and torque limits.
- Environmental conditions.
- Safety.

Payload capacity alone does not guarantee successful execution.
