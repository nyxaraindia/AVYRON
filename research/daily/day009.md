## 1. Research Objective

Investigate how AVYRON should distinguish a robot's original design from its current hardware condition and available capabilities.

The central research question:

**If a robot's hardware becomes damaged during a task, how should AVYRON update its understanding without changing the robot's original design information?**

## 2. Research Scenario

Robot: HEXA-R01

Original configuration:

- Six legs.
- Eight motors.
- Two cameras.
- One LiDAR.
- Battery: 90%.

During a task:

- One leg actuator becomes damaged.
- One camera becomes disconnected.
- Battery drops to 35%.
- The robot is carrying a 2 kg object.

### Question 4: What should AVYRON do before allowing the robot to continue carrying the object?

**User's answer:**

"Stop the work."

**Evaluation:** Correct.

Research interpretation:

AVYRON should stop the current task and prevent further movement until the robot's condition and load situation have been assessed.

A safe response should include:

1. Stop task execution.
2. Enter or maintain a safe holding state if possible.
3. Assess the damaged leg and robot stability.
4. Evaluate the 2 kg carried load.
5. Check battery condition and available sensors.
6. Request independent safety authorization before recovery or further movement.
7. Notify the operator if the robot cannot safely continue.

**Important:** Stopping the task does not automatically mean dropping the carried object. AVYRON must assess how to secure the load safely.

## 4. Research Principle: Design vs Runtime State

This research practical supports a distinction between three different concepts.

### A. Original Configuration

Describes what the robot was designed and built to contain.

Examples:

- Six legs.
- Eight motors.
- Two cameras.
- One LiDAR.
- Original physical dimensions.

This information should not be overwritten just because a component becomes damaged.

### B. Current Hardware Condition

Describes the actual condition of the robot's components.

Examples:

- One actuator damaged.
- One camera disconnected.
- Battery health.
- Sensor operational status.

This information must be updated when the system detects verified changes.

### C. Current Available Capabilities

Describes what the robot can currently perform, considering its hardware condition and operating circumstances.

Examples:

- Normal walking may be unavailable.
- Obstacle detection through the disconnected camera is unavailable.
- LiDAR-based perception may remain available if verified operational.
- Carrying the object may require reassessment.

These capabilities must not be assumed solely from the original robot specifications.

## 6. Important Architectural Principle

**Designed Capability ≠ Available Capability ≠ Verified Safe Capability**

These concepts must remain separate.

- Designed capability: What the robot was originally built to do.
- Available capability: What its currently functioning hardware and software can support.
- Verified safe capability: What has been assessed and authorized under the current conditions.

A capability may exist in the original design but become unavailable because of damage, environmental conditions or resource limitations.

## 8. Research Conclusions

The research practical supports these preliminary design principles:

1. Original robot configuration should be preserved.
2. Hardware condition should be tracked separately.
3. Current capabilities must reflect operational changes.
4. AVYRON must not assume that original capabilities remain available.
5. Task execution should stop when a fault makes safe continuation uncertain.
6. A carried load must be considered during fault handling.
7. Independent safety authorization must govern recovery and further movement.
