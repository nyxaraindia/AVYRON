## 1. Research Objective

Understand the fundamental intelligence loop used in robotics.

Investigate how a robot:

1. Receives information from sensors.
2. Interprets the environment.
3. Selects actions.
4. Executes physical actions.
5. Observes the results.
6. Updates its understanding.

Connect this process to the proposed AVYRON universal robotics intelligence architecture.

## 2. Main Research Question

How can a universal robotic intelligence system continuously perceive, understand, decide, act and adapt across different robot embodiments?

Supporting questions:

- How should sensor information be represented?
- How can perception be separated from understanding?
- How should a robot evaluate possible actions?
- How should feedback update its world model?
- How can safety remain independent of autonomous decisions?
- How can the same intelligence architecture support different robots?

## 3. Topics Investigated

### 3.1 Perception

Perception is the process of receiving and processing information from the environment and the robot's internal sensors.

Examples:

- Cameras
- LiDAR
- Ultrasonic sensors
- IMU
- Microphones
- Force sensors
- Touch sensors

Research observation:

Different embodiments may have different sensor configurations.

A universal architecture must not assume that every robot has the same sensors.

### 3.2 Understanding

Understanding involves interpreting information and estimating the current state of the environment.

Examples:

- Object recognition.
- Obstacle identification.
- Position estimation.
- Human detection.
- Environment interpretation.
- Robot state estimation.

Research observation:

Sensor detection and semantic understanding are separate problems.

An object detector may identify an object without understanding its relevance to the current task.

### 3.3 Decision-Making

Decision-making selects actions based on the robot's current understanding and objectives.

Relevant considerations:

- Goal requirements.
- Available capabilities.
- Physical limitations.
- Environmental conditions.
- Resources.
- Uncertainty.
- Safety constraints.

Research observation:

An intelligent planner should evaluate whether an action is feasible for the current robot and its current state.

### 3.4 Action

Action converts decisions into commands that can be executed by the robot.

Examples:

- Motor commands.
- Joint movement.
- Gripper control.
- Mobile navigation.
- Drone flight control.

Research observation:

The same abstract task may require different physical actions on different embodiments.

### 3.5 Feedback

Feedback provides information about the result of an action.

Examples:

- Position changes.
- Motor response.
- Object movement.
- Obstacle distance.
- Task completion.
- Unexpected physical behaviour.

Research observation:

Feedback is essential for detecting differences between expected and actual results.

## 6. Important Research Observations

### Observation 1 — Perception is not understanding

Receiving information does not guarantee correct interpretation.

A camera may detect an object, but the robot must estimate what the object is and how it relates to the current task.

### Observation 2 — Understanding can be uncertain

Sensors can produce incomplete or contradictory information.

The intelligence system should represent uncertainty rather than always assuming that its interpretation is correct.

### Observation 3 — Decisions depend on current conditions

A robot's capabilities may change because of:

- Battery level.
- Sensor damage.
- Motor temperature.
- Payload.
- Mechanical faults.
- Environmental changes.

Therefore, an action that was feasible earlier may no longer be feasible.

### Observation 4 — Actions require embodiment-specific execution

A humanoid, robotic arm, quadruped and drone cannot use identical motor commands.

A common intelligence layer needs embodiment-specific interfaces.

### Observation 5 — Feedback enables adaptation

The robot must compare expected results with observed results.

Unexpected outcomes should trigger appropriate reassessment, replanning or safe stopping.

### Observation 6 — Safety must remain independently enforced

The intelligence system must not be able to authorize itself to ignore safety constraints.

The safety architecture requires independent enforcement mechanisms and appropriate validation.

## 7. Proposed Research Hypotheses

These are hypotheses to investigate, not proven findings.

### Hypothesis H1

A common world-state representation could help different robot embodiments use shared reasoning and planning components.

### Hypothesis H2

Explicit uncertainty representation could improve the reliability of decisions made from incomplete or conflicting sensor information.

### Hypothesis H3

Continuous feedback and state updates could improve task execution when environmental conditions change.

### Hypothesis H4

Separating abstract task decisions from embodiment-specific control could improve the portability of the intelligence architecture.

### Hypothesis H5

Independent safety enforcement could reduce the risk of unsafe actions caused by incorrect planning or learning.

- A wheeled robot.
