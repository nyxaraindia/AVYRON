# AVYRON — Day 1 Research Log

## Research Objective

Investigate why universal robotic intelligence must understand
the capabilities and limitations of each robot before assigning tasks.

---

## 1. Research Question

What information must AVYRON know about a robot before deciding
whether it can safely perform a requested task?

## 2. Research Scenario

Instruction:

"Pick up the red box and place it on the table."

Robots considered:

1. Humanoid.
2. Robotic arm.
3. Drone.

The task goal is shared, but the physical capabilities differ.

AVYRON must not assume that every robot can perform the same actions.

## 3. Initial Capability Categories

| Capability | Information to investigate |
|---|---|
| Gripper | Can it securely hold the object? |
| Vision | Can it detect the object? |
| Movement | Can it reach the object and destination? |
| Strength | Can it handle the object's weight? |
| Environment | Are operating conditions suitable? |
| Safety | Can the task be performed safely? |

These are initial categories, not a complete formal capability model.

## 4. Payload Reasoning Experiment

Given:

- Humanoid capacity: 5 kg.
- Robotic arm capacity: 15 kg.
- Drone capacity: 2 kg.
- Box weight: 8 kg.

### Humanoid

8 - 5 = 3 kg above the stated capacity.

Decision: Reject the lifting action under the given limit.

### Drone

8 - 2 = 6 kg above the stated capacity.

Decision: Reject the lifting action under the given limit.

### Robotic Arm

15 - 8 = 7 kg difference.

Decision: Select as a candidate for further verification.

The robotic arm must still pass additional checks:

1. Gripper suitability.
2. Reachability.
3. Workspace limits.
4. Force and torque limits.
5. Environmental conditions.
6. Safety constraints.

A payload rating alone does not prove that the complete task is feasible.

These numbers are illustrative, not manufacturer specifications.

## 5. Initial Research Finding

### Capability-Aware Task Assignment

Proposed principle:

Before assigning a task, AVYRON should compare the task's physical
requirements against the robot's verified capabilities and current
operating conditions.

Proposed process:

1. Identify task requirements.
2. Obtain robot capability information.
3. Check required capabilities.
4. Check quantitative physical limits.
5. Consider environmental conditions.
6. Verify safety constraints.
7. Reject infeasible actions.
8. Consider alternative strategies or robots.

This is a research concept, not a claim of scientific originality.

Prior-art investigation is required.

## 6. Safety Observation

Learning and adaptive planning must not bypass independent safety enforcement.

The intelligence engine may propose a strategy, but an independent
safety authority must be able to reject unsafe commands.

## 7. Candidate Research Hypothesis

H1:

A capability-aware task assignment mechanism may reduce invalid
robot-task assignments across heterogeneous robot embodiments.

This hypothesis requires experimental validation.

## 8. Possible Evaluation Metrics

- Invalid task assignment rate.
- Task success rate.
- Physical constraint violations.
- Safety violations.
- Time required to select a suitable robot.
- Performance across different embodiments.
- Correct rejection of infeasible tasks.

## 9. Open Research Questions

1. How should AVYRON represent robot capabilities?
2. How should it represent uncertainty in observations?
3. How can it verify that capability information is current?
4. How should it compare different physical capabilities?
5. How should it handle changing robot conditions?
6. How should it respond when no robot can perform a task?
7. How should safety constraints be independently enforced?

## 10. Research Decisions

- Investigate capability-aware reasoning first.
- Treat robot embodiments as physically different systems.
- Never assume capability based on appearance.
- Require feasibility checks before execution.
- Keep safety enforcement independent of learning.
- Validate claims against existing robotics research.
