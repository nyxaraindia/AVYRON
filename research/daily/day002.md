# AVYRON — Day 2 Research Log

## Research Objective

Investigate how a universal robotic intelligence architecture
can describe robot capabilities, sensors, actuators, physical
constraints and operational resources.

---

## 1. Research Question

How should AVYRON represent a robot's capabilities so that the
same intelligence system can understand humanoids, robotic arms,
drones and future robot embodiments?

---

## 2. Important Distinctions

The lesson introduced five different concepts:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hardware | Physical components |
| Capability | What the robot can do |
| Limitation | Restrictions on its abilities |
| Condition | Current operational state |
| Environment | Surroundings affecting operation |

These concepts should not be treated as interchangeable.

A robot can have a physical component but still be unable to use
it because of damage, energy limitations or environmental conditions.

---

## 3. Sensor Research Notes

Sensors provide information about the environment or the robot.

Examples:

- Camera: visual information.
- LiDAR: distance and surrounding geometry.
- Ultrasonic: distance measurement.
- Microphone: sound.
- IMU: acceleration and angular motion.
- Force sensor: applied force.
- Touch sensor: physical contact.

Research observation:

A camera does not directly measure an object's weight.

Visual observations and physical measurements must be distinguished.

Potential research direction:

Represent the type and source of information available to AVYRON,
rather than treating every observation as equally reliable.

---

## 4. Actuator Research Notes

Actuators produce physical actions.

Examples:

- Servo motors.
- DC motors.
- Linear actuators.
- Grippers.
- Drone motors.

Research observation:

A robot's ability to act depends on its actuators, control system,
physical configuration and current condition.

## 7. Proposed Research Concept

### Robot Capability Profile

A proposed representation that describes what a robot has,
what it can do, its limits and its available resources.

Illustrative structure:

ROBOT_CAPABILITY_PROFILE {

    IDENTITY

    SENSORS

    ACTUATORS

    PERCEPTION

    MOBILITY

    MANIPULATION

    PHYSICAL_LIMITS

    COMPUTATIONAL_RESOURCES

    ENERGY_RESOURCES

    CURRENT_CONDITION

    ENVIRONMENT

    UNCERTAINTY

    SAFETY_CONSTRAINTS

}

This is an early conceptual structure.

It is not the final AVYRON data model or language specification.

## 8. Practical Reasoning Experiment

Robot:

- Camera: Available.
- Gripper: Available.
- Wheels: Available.
- Maximum payload: 3 kg.
- Maximum speed: 1 m/s.
- LiDAR: Not available.

Task:

Find a 2 kg box, pick it up and transport it.

### Learner's Responses

1. Visual search: Yes.
2. Picking up the box: Yes.
3. Transporting the box: Yes.
4. Missing information: Distance it can reach.
5. Guarantee of success: Yes, partially.

### Research Review

The camera makes visual search possible in principle,
but detection performance depends on the camera and environment.

The 2 kg object is below the stated 3 kg payload limit.
However, gripper suitability must still be checked.

Wheels provide mobility, but carrying the box also requires
checking stability, motor capacity, terrain and route conditions.

The learner correctly identified reachability as missing information.

Other unknowns include battery level, gripper force, terrain,
obstacles and sensor reliability.

Conclusion:

The task appears potentially feasible, but successful execution
cannot be guaranteed from the available specifications.

---

## 9. Research Finding

### Feasibility Is Not the Same as Guaranteed Success

A task may satisfy known capability limits while still failing
because of unknown conditions, faulty components, uncertain
observations or unexpected environmental changes.

Potential AVYRON requirements:

1. Represent available capabilities.
2. Represent quantitative limits.
3. Represent current operational information.
4. Identify missing information.
5. Account for uncertainty.
6. Reject tasks that violate known constraints.
7. Monitor execution and verify outcomes.

---

## 10. Candidate Research Hypotheses

### H1 — Capability Representation

A structured robot capability representation may help one
intelligence architecture reason across heterogeneous embodiments.

### H2 — Operational State Awareness

Task feasibility decisions may improve when the system considers
the robot's current operational state in addition to its hardware.

### H3 — Uncertainty Awareness

Explicitly representing missing or uncertain information may reduce
unsupported task-feasibility decisions.

These are hypotheses, not established results.

They require prior-art review and experimental validation.

5. How should missing information be represented?
6. How should uncertainty be measured?

---

## 13. Research Decisions

- Separate hardware, capability, limitation, condition and environment.
- Distinguish visual observations from direct physical measurements.
- Include quantitative constraints in capability reasoning.
- Do not treat payload compatibility as proof of task success.
- Investigate operational-state-aware capability profiles.
- Represent uncertainty and missing information.
- Keep safety verification independent of learning.
